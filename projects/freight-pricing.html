Making freight pricing simpler for customers.

An Excel-based self-service calculator designed to help customers access applicable freight rates from their existing quotations without repeatedly contacting the Local Sales team.

Excel Pricing Logic Process Improvement Customer Self-Service Local Sales

01 / The challenge

Rates were available. Accessing them took extra effort.

Customers who had already received a quotation frequently contacted Local Sales by phone or email to ask for a rate covered by that quotation. Each enquiry required additional communication and repeated manual work.

THE CUSTOMER

Repeated enquiries

Customers needed a straightforward way to check applicable rates for their shipments without having to contact their sales representative every time.

THE SALES TEAM

Repetitive manual work

Sales representatives spent time responding to rate enquiries and looking up information that had already been provided through customer quotations.

02 / My approach

A practical idea based on first-hand experience.

After two years in a sales role where I personally handled shipment pricing enquiries by email, I recognised an opportunity to simplify the process. I independently came up with the idea and built the original Excel calculator from scratch.

01. Understand the problem Identify repeated rate enquiries and unnecessary manual steps.
02. Structure the solution Organise customer-specific rates and define the lookup logic.
03. Build the calculator Create an Excel tool customers could use independently.

03 / The solution

A customer-specific Excel pricing tool.

After preparing a quotation, the sales representative could provide the customer with a calculator based on the applicable rates. Customers entered shipment details, while Excel formulas looked up the matching rate from a separate, hidden worksheet containing the rate table.

Customer-specific rate table

Applicable rates were stored on a separate hidden worksheet within the workbook. Each customer received dedicated price list, created just for them.

Formula-based lookup

Excel lookup formulas, such as INDEX or VLOOKUP, retrieved the relevant rate from the stored table.

Shipment parameters

Customers entered pick-up and destination postal codes, weight, dimensions and pallet count.

Calculated pricing

The calculator applied the relevant rate logic to the shipment parameters to return the applicable price.

04 / The impact

Less repetitive work. Qucik decisions.

The calculator gave customers served by Local Sales a practical way to check rates already covered by their quotations, while reducing the need for repeated manual enquiries.

FOR CUSTOMERS

Self-service access

  • Check applicable rates independently.
  • Reduce the need for repeated phone calls and emails.
  • Straight forward to place a booking, instead of waiting for quotation.
FOR LOCAL SALES

Less repetitive manual work

  • Spend less time answering repeated rate enquiries.
  • Reduce manual rate lookups and related error risk.
  • Make more efficient use of sales team time.

From local initiative to a broader concept.

The original calculator was my own solution for customers served by Local Sales. Management later became aware of the concept, which was adopted more broadly within the company. The wider implementation was delivered by IT; I am not claiming ownership of that later implementation.

Turning a recurring business problem into a usable tool.

This project demonstrates how first-hand operational experience can reveal opportunities for process improvement — and how a practical, independently developed solution can make an everyday business process simpler for both customers and colleagues.
